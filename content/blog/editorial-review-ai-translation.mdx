# Why Editorial Review Matters in AI Book Translation

AI translation has changed the economics of publishing in multiple languages. What used to cost $5,000–$20,000 per language can now start at $99. But speed and price only matter if the final book reads like a book — not a machine output.

That's where editorial review comes in.

## The Problem With Raw AI Translation

Raw AI translation is impressive, but it has blind spots:

- **Idioms** get translated literally
- **Cultural context** gets flattened
- **Character voice** becomes generic
- **Regional differences** are ignored (Spain vs. Latin America, Portugal vs. Brazil)

A thriller where every police term is translated into local equivalents can feel like it moved countries mid-scene. A romance where every endearment is varied inconsistently can break the intimacy between characters.

## What Editorial Review Fixes

BookLingua runs a second pass over every translation. A premium AI editor looks at the whole manuscript and adjusts:

- Tone and register
- Idioms and natural phrasing
- Consistency of names, terms, and style
- Regional language choices for your target market

Every change is highlighted in yellow so you can review, accept, or adjust.

## A Real Example

In a recent Spanish translation, "He was read his Miranda rights" became:

> "Le leyeron sus derechos Miranda"

A literal translation would have lost the American legal context. We kept "Miranda" as a globally recognised term and added a translation note explaining the decision.
