If you’ve ever looked into translating your book, you’ve probably discovered that working out how much it will cost isn’t always straightforward.

Prices vary because every quote includes something slightly different. The length and complexity of your book matter, as does the language you choose and the amount of editing or proofreading included. One provider may quote a few hundred dollars, while another comes back with a figure closer to the advance on a publishing deal.

The short answer is that translating a full-length book can cost anything from under $100 for a very basic AI-generated translation to $20,000 or more for a specialist human translation. The important question isn’t simply which option is cheapest. It’s what you get for your money and whether the result will be ready to publish.

## How are book translation costs calculated?

Most professional translators calculate their fee using the word count of the original manuscript. This makes more sense than charging by page because a 300-page paperback with generous spacing may contain far fewer words than a densely written nonfiction book of the same length.

The basic calculation is straightforward:

> **Example: 80,000 words × $0.15 per word = $12,000 for the translation.**

But that figure might only cover the translator’s work. Depending on the agreement, you may also need to budget for a second editor, proofreading, formatting, translated marketing copy or a royalty arrangement. Make sure you always check what is included before comparing two quotes.

## How much does professional human book translation cost?

Translating a book properly takes time and skill. A literary translator isn’t simply swapping one word for another. They are recreating the book in a new language while trying to preserve its meaning, voice, humour and character.

Published rates vary widely. [The Literary Translators’ Association of Canada](https://www.attlc-ltac.org/en/rates/) currently recommends C$0.25 per word for genres other than poetry and drama. A current guide from translation platform [Smartling](https://www.smartling.com/blog/translation-rates) places professional US translation rates around US$0.15 to $0.30 per word, although book projects are individually quoted and the language pair and scope make a significant difference.

For an 80,000-word novel, that creates a rough translation budget of:

- $12,000 at $0.15 per word
- $16,000 at $0.20 per word
- $20,000 at $0.25 per word

For indie authors, spending five figures on a single language can be difficult to justify when you don’t know how many sales you’re going to make.

## What affects the cost of translating a book?

### 1. Word count

Longer books generally cost more. This is why it’s worth using the final, edited manuscript when requesting a quote. Translating a draft and then translating substantial revisions can add unnecessary cost and create version-control problems.

### 2. The language pair

Some languages cost more than others, largely because there are fewer suitable translators available. A translator working from English into Spanish may be easier to find than someone working with a less common language combination. The direction matters too: translating into English is a different market from translating out of English.

### 3. Genre and complexity

Some books are simply more complicated to translate. A contemporary novel may need less research than a medical textbook, a historical novel full of period language or a fantasy series with its own mythology.

That doesn’t mean fiction is always easy. Romance, humour and children’s books depend heavily on voice, wordplay and emotional tone, all of which can be surprisingly difficult to carry into another language.

### 4. Editing and proofreading

Some quotes include an editor or proofreader while others cover only the first translation. A second native-language professional can check fluency, consistency and obvious errors, but a full line-by-line edit is a much larger piece of work than a final proofreading check.

### 5. Timescale

Urgent work usually costs more, and a rushed schedule may limit your choice of translator.

### 6. Rights and royalties

Literary translators sometimes negotiate royalties, an advance, a credit on the book and other contractual rights alongside the translation fee. If you are commissioning a translator directly, agree in writing who owns the translated text, where it can be published and how the translator will be credited.

## What other costs should authors budget for?

The manuscript is only one part of a translated edition. Depending on how much you do yourself, your wider budget may also include:

- A native-language edit or final proofread
- A translated or redesigned cover
- Ebook and paperback formatting
- Translation and localisation of the title, description and author biography
- Keyword and category research for the local marketplace
- Advance reader copies or a local reader panel
- Advertising, promotional sites and launch support
- A new ISBN, where one is required for that edition and format

This is easy to overlook. A beautifully translated book with a literal description, unsuitable keywords and no launch plan can still struggle to find readers. It’s sensible to keep some of your budget back for publishing and promoting the new edition, rather than spending everything on the translation itself.

## Can you translate a book with AI for free?

Technically, yes. You can paste sections of a manuscript into a general-purpose AI or machine-translation tool and generate text at little or no direct cost.

But—and it’s a big but—you still need to manage chapter structure, character names, invented terminology, formatting, context limits, regional language, omissions, duplicated passages and quality control. If you do not read the target language, it can be very difficult to judge whether the result is accurate, readable or even accidentally hilarious.

For a short internal document, a rough translation may be enough. But if the book will carry your name and be sold to readers, you need a more reliable process than simply pasting it into an AI tool and hoping for the best.

## Is translating your book worth the cost?

The answer depends on the book and the route you choose. Start by looking at the commercial case rather than translating into a language simply because it has a large population.

Think about:

- Whether books in your genre sell in that language
- How similar titles are priced and reviewed
- Whether your existing book has already proved it can attract readers
- Whether you can reach readers through ads, newsletters or local promotions
- Whether the translation can support several books in a series rather than one isolated title
- How many copies you would need to sell to recover the full production and launch cost

If a translated ebook earns $3 in royalty per sale, a $12,000 translation requires roughly 4,000 sales just to recover the translation fee, before cover, formatting and marketing costs. A $149 service has a much lower break-even point, although you should still account for the time and money needed to finish and launch the edition.

For many indie authors, that difference changes translation from a major gamble into a market test. If the first book performs well, you can reinvest in stronger localisation, more human editorial support and the rest of the series.

## How to compare book translation quotes

Before choosing the lowest number on the page, ask each provider:

- Is the price based on the source or translated word count?
- Does it include editing and proofreading?
- What level of professional translator review is included, and how are the passages selected?
- Will the provider act on the translator’s feedback before delivering the manuscript?
- How are missing text, duplicated passages and formatting errors checked?
- Will names, terminology and character voices remain consistent across the book?
- Are the title, description and marketing materials included?
- What files will I receive, and will the formatting be preserved?
- Who owns the translation, and are royalties or additional rights involved?
- What happens if the translation fails the agreed quality checks?

A cheap quote can become expensive if you later need to rebuild the manuscript or pay for extensive editing. Equally, the most comprehensive service may be more than a new market test requires. The best option is the one that gives you enough confidence in the finished book without spending more than the opportunity can realistically justify.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does it cost to translate a 300-page book?

Page counts vary, so use the word count where possible. If a 300-page book contains around 75,000 words, a human translation at $0.15–$0.25 per word could cost roughly $11,250–$18,750. An AI-assisted service may cost far less, but the workflow and human review will be different.

### How long does it take to translate a book?

A professional human translation may take several months, depending on the book and the translator’s schedule. Specialist AI-assisted workflows can complete the main translation much faster, but authors should still allow time for review, formatting and preparation of the new edition.

### Does a book translator receive royalties?

Sometimes. Literary translation agreements may include a flat fee, royalties or both. The translator may also have contractual rights and credit requirements. Agree the terms in writing before work begins.

### Is AI book translation good enough to publish?

It can be, but the process matters. A raw output from a general AI tool is not the same as a workflow that checks structure, consistency, style and readability, then sends selected difficult passages for review by a professional translator. Check whether the provider acts on that feedback before delivering the manuscript.

### What is the cheapest way to translate a book?

A general-purpose AI tool has the lowest direct cost, but it leaves the author responsible for managing and checking the entire project. For authors who do not read the target language, a specialist service with targeted review by a professional translator offers more reassurance while remaining far cheaper than a full human translation.

### Does BookLingua include a full human proofread?

No. BookLingua selects passages where professional judgement is most valuable and sends those for targeted review by a professional translator. It then acts on relevant feedback and provides the updated translation with the full report. This is not a line-by-line human proofread of the complete manuscript.
